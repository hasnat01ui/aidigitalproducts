# AUREVIA — Competitor Analysis

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Market:** AI systems and tooling for small agencies and independent consultants

---

## 0. Honesty statement on method

This analysis is built from public marketing pages, category research, and published pricing guides. It is **desk research, not a teardown** — no products were purchased, and no vendor was contacted.

Two rules were applied throughout:

1. **Prices marked `~` are approximate or published ranges and must be re-verified before being used in any public comparison.** Competitor pricing changes frequently, and publishing a wrong number is both a credibility and a legal risk.
2. **No fabricated reviews or complaints.** Where "common complaints" appear, they are patterns reported in category discussion, described as such — never invented quotes attributed to real customers.

---

## 1. The competitive set is four layers, not one

AUREVIA's real competition is not a list of similar products — no direct equivalent was found. It is four *categories* that each solve part of the problem, plus the status quo.

| Layer | What it is | Price band | Why the buyer chooses it | Why they leave |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Raw AI tools** | ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini | ~$20–30/user/mo | Universal, cheap, already owned | Shapeless. No workflow, no standard, no repeatability |
| **B. Generic AI templates** | Prompt packs, Notion/Gumroad templates | ~$9–79 | Cheap, instant | Bought and abandoned. No system, no outcome |
| **C. Agency platforms with AI bolted on** | Notion AI, ClickUp AI, Service Provider Pro, Zendo | ~$10–40/user/mo | Already in the stack | AI is a feature, not a method. Still a blank page |
| **D. Done-for-you AI consultancies** | AI automation agencies | ~$3k–8k/mo retainer, ~$3k–10k setup; fixed-scope builds ~$8k–35k | Someone else does it | Priced out of reach for a 5-person agency |

**The structural gap:** between (B) at ~$50 and (D) at ~$5,000/month there is almost nothing. A small agency that needs a real system but cannot spend consultancy money has no good option. That gap is AUREVIA's entry point.

## 2. The ten competitors

### 1. ChatGPT (OpenAI) — *the true default*

- **Product:** general assistant, Projects, custom GPTs
- **Price:** ~$20/user/mo (Plus), ~$25–30/seat (Team)
- **Audience:** everyone
- **Promise:** "AI that can do anything"
- **Strengths:** ubiquitous, trusted, capable, already paid for. Custom GPTs partially overlap AUREVIA's offer.
- **Weaknesses:** anything-shaped is nobody-shaped. No agency domain knowledge, no delivery standard, no team consistency. The blank page is the problem.
- **Common complaints in category discussion:** inconsistent output between team members; no institutional memory of "how we do it here".
- **Marketing:** brand ubiquity; no need to compete
- **Threat to AUREVIA:** **Highest.** The honest answer to "why not just use ChatGPT?" must be the first thing the homepage answers. AUREVIA is not a replacement for it — AUREVIA is the system that runs *on top of* it. That framing must be explicit, not implied.

### 2. Claude (Anthropic)

- **Price:** ~$20/user/mo; Team seats higher
- **Strengths:** strong long-document and writing quality; Projects for persistent context
- **Weaknesses:** same as above — a capable engine, not a method
- **Threat:** High, and identical in shape to ChatGPT. Also a *dependency*: AUREVIA's system should be model-agnostic so it works with whichever assistant the agency already pays for. That is a feature, not a compromise.

### 3. Notion AI

- **Price:** ~$10–20/user/mo on top of a Notion plan
- **Audience:** teams already living in Notion — a large share of small agencies
- **Promise:** AI inside your existing workspace; generate SOPs, meeting notes, onboarding docs
- **Strengths:** zero switching cost; strong overlap with the SOP/documentation use case
- **Weaknesses:** generic generation. Produces *a* document, not the agency's document to the agency's standard.
- **Threat:** **High and under-appreciated.** Notion AI can plausibly satisfy the buyer's felt need. AUREVIA's counter is depth of method — and, tactically, *shipping the system as Notion-compatible* rather than fighting the incumbent workspace.

### 4. ClickUp AI / Asana AI / Monday AI

- **Price:** ~$7–20/user/mo add-on
- **Strengths:** embedded in the tool where work already happens; task and status automation
- **Weaknesses:** aimed at project *management*, not creative or strategic *delivery*
- **Threat:** Medium. Adjacent rather than overlapping.

### 5. Service Provider Pro (SPP)

- **Product:** agency platform — service catalog, client portal, billing, CRM
- **Price:** ~$130+/mo (verify)
- **Audience:** productized-service agencies
- **Strengths:** genuinely agency-native; solves ordering and client management well
- **Weaknesses:** manages the *business* of the agency, not the *production* of the work. Does not make the deliverable.
- **Threat:** Low, overlap is thin. **Better read as a partnership or integration target than a competitor.**

### 6. Zendo

- **Product:** productized-services platform — scoped offers, order forms, service catalog
- **Price:** ~$29–99/mo (verify)
- **Strengths:** clean fit for agencies packaging fixed-scope offers
- **Weaknesses:** transactional layer only; no AI delivery method
- **Threat:** Low. Same read as SPP.

### 7. AI automation agencies / consultancies (category)

- **Price:** ~$3k–8k/mo retainer plus ~$3k–10k setup; fixed-scope builds ~$8k–35k
- **Promise:** "we will build your AI operations for you"
- **Strengths:** fully done-for-you; custom; real outcomes for those who can pay
- **Weaknesses:** priced far beyond the target buyer; long engagements; the client owns a build they cannot maintain
- **Common complaints in category discussion:** unclear ROI, dependency on the vendor, deliverables that decay once the engagement ends
- **Threat:** Low as a competitor for AUREVIA's buyer — **but high as a signal.** These prices prove the problem is worth real money. AUREVIA is the self-serve tier of a market that has already validated the pain at 20× the price.

### 8. Prompt marketplaces (PromptBase and similar)

- **Price:** ~$2–10 per prompt
- **Strengths:** cheap, enormous selection
- **Weaknesses:** commodity. No workflow, no support, no updates, no outcome.
- **Threat:** Low on merit, **medium on perception** — they have trained buyers to think "AI prompts" means "cheap". AUREVIA must not be filed in this category, which is a naming, packaging, and positioning problem as much as a product one.

### 9. Gumroad / Etsy AI template sellers (category)

- **Price:** ~$9–99
- **Strengths:** volume, social proof, fast iteration
- **Weaknesses:** thin, disposable, largely unmaintained
- **Threat:** Low on quality, **high on price anchoring.** They set the buyer's reference price for "an AI product", and AUREVIA intends to charge 5–10× that. Overcoming that anchor is a core positioning task, not an afterthought.

### 10. Doing nothing / building it in-house

- **Price:** free, plus the owner's time
- **Strengths:** no spend, no risk, feels controllable
- **Weaknesses:** never finished. The Notion page of prompts started in month one and abandoned by month two is the archetype.
- **Threat:** **Highest after ChatGPT.** In this market the most common outcome is not losing to a competitor — it is the buyer deciding to sort it out themselves and never doing it. Marketing must beat inertia, not rivals.

## 3. Competitive summary

| Rank | Competitor | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ChatGPT / Claude as-is | Highest |
| 2 | Doing nothing / DIY | Highest |
| 3 | Notion AI | High |
| 4 | Template/prompt sellers (price anchoring) | Medium |
| 5 | ClickUp / Asana / Monday AI | Medium |
| 6 | AI consultancies | Low (different tier) |
| 7 | SPP / Zendo | Low (adjacent) |

**The most important conclusion:** AUREVIA's competition is not other products. It is **the buyer's belief that they can figure this out themselves with the AI tool they already pay for.** Every piece of positioning, copy, and content must be built against that belief — honestly, by demonstrating the depth of method they would have to build, not by disparaging the tools they already use.
