## 0. Honesty statement

This avatar is constructed from secondary research and reasoning about how small agencies operate. **No customer interviews have been conducted.** Every claim below is a testable assumption, and the ones that matter most are listed in §14. Nothing here may be published as evidence of demand.

## 2. Secondary avatar — "the independent consultant"

Solo strategist, fractional CMO/CTO, or specialist consultant billing $75–$250/hour. Same pain, smaller scale, faster decision (no team to convince). Useful as an easier first customer, but with lower lifetime value than the agency owner.

## 3. Goals

1. Increase profit **without** hiring — headcount is the thing that ate their margin last time.
2. Deliver client work faster and more consistently, so quality does not depend on who happened to do the task.
3. Stop being the bottleneck personally. Get out of the delivery weeds and into growth.
4. Win more of the proposals they write, and waste less time on the ones they lose.
5. Look sophisticated and modern to clients who are themselves asking about AI.

## 4. Problems

- Non-billable work (proposals, scoping, onboarding, status reports, recaps, QA) consumes 25–40% of the team's week and generates no revenue.
- Every project starts from a blank page. Nothing compounds; the tenth project takes nearly as long as the first.
- Quality varies by person. The owner ends up reviewing everything, which is the real bottleneck.
- Clients are starting to ask "what are you doing with AI?" and there is no credible answer.
- The team is experimenting with AI individually and inconsistently — different tools, different prompts, uneven output, occasional embarrassment.
- Capacity is capped by hours, so revenue is capped by hours.

## 5. Pain points, ranked by intensity

1. **"I am turning down work because we are at capacity, but I cannot afford to hire."** — the sharpest, most monetary pain.
2. **"I spend my evenings writing proposals and reports instead of running the business."**
3. **"Onboarding a new client takes two weeks of chaos every single time."**
4. **"I cannot delegate quality — everything routes back through me."**
5. **"We lost a pitch to an agency that talked a better AI story than we do."**

## 6. Frustrations with the current alternatives

- **Generic AI tools:** powerful but shapeless. "ChatGPT can do anything" is useless to someone who needs *this specific deliverable, at our standard, every time.*
- **Prompt packs:** bought one, skimmed it, never opened it again. No workflow, no integration, no outcome.
- **Enterprise AI platforms:** priced and scoped for companies 50× larger. Long implementations, seat minimums, sales calls.
- **Courses:** learned the theory, still has no system on Monday morning. Education without an artefact does not change behaviour.
- **Hiring an AI consultant:** $10k+ and produces a slide deck, not a working system.
- **Building it themselves:** started a Notion page of prompts, abandoned it in three weeks. No time to build the thing that would give them time.

## 7. Current solutions in use

ChatGPT / Claude (unstructured), Notion or Google Docs, a project tool (Asana / ClickUp / Monday), Slack, canned proposal software, a scattering of scraped prompts in a doc nobody maintains.

**The gap:** they own the AI. They do not own a *system* for using it. That gap — between access and results — is precisely where money is being spent in this market.

## 8. Buying objections

| Objection | What is really being asked |
|---|---|
| "Is this just another prompt pack?" | Will this actually change how we work, or is it a PDF? |
| "Will my team actually use it?" | Adoption is the real risk, not the purchase price. |
| "Our work is bespoke — will this fit us?" | Is this rigid, or does it adapt to our services? |
| "How long until it pays for itself?" | Give me the arithmetic. |
| "Will this be stale in six months?" | Models change fast. Is this maintained? |
| "Can I trust AI output in front of a client?" | Quality control and review are non-negotiable. |
| "Who are you?" | No track record, no testimonials, no case studies. **This is currently AUREVIA's biggest objection and it is legitimate.** |

## 9. Desired outcome

> "My agency delivers more client work, at a consistent standard, without more hours and without more headcount — and I get my evenings back."

Concretely, what a win looks like to them: proposals in 30 minutes instead of 4 hours; onboarding as a repeatable sequence instead of improvisation; first drafts produced by the system and refined by humans; the owner reviewing rather than producing.

## 10. Willingness to pay

| Offer | Anchor price | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Free lead magnet | $0 | Must deliver a real win to earn the email |
| Starter product | $49–$99 | Single workflow, solves one painful task |
| Core product | $199–$499 | The full system. Justified if it saves ~10 hrs/month against a $75–150/hr rate |
| Premium / done-with-you | $799–$1,500 | Includes implementation support |
| Aurevia Pro (recurring) | $29–$79/month | Ongoing updates, new workflows, member resources |

The economic argument is simple and needs no exaggeration: at $100/hour, ten hours saved per month is $1,000/month of recovered capacity. A $299 product clears that in its first month. **This is the honest sales argument, and it is strong enough that no hype is required** — which matters, given the brand's prohibition on fake urgency.

## 13. Trust factors

In rough order of weight for this buyer:

1. **Demonstrated depth** — content that proves AUREVIA understands agency operations specifically, not "AI" generally. This is the one trust factor available on day one, before any customers exist.
2. **Show, don't claim** — real screen recordings of the system working end to end.
3. **Specificity** — named workflows and concrete numbers beat adjectives.
4. **A real refund policy** — plainly stated, honoured.
5. **A visible human founder** — a name and a face, not a faceless brand.
6. **Free value first** — the lead magnet must be genuinely useful standalone.
7. Testimonials and case studies — **the strongest factor, and AUREVIA has none.** Per the no-fake-claims rule these will not be fabricated; they will be earned from the first cohort. Until then, points 1–6 carry the weight, and the first customers should be recruited on an explicit founding-customer basis.

## 14. Assumptions that must be validated

Ranked by how much damage a wrong answer does:

1. **Agency owners believe non-billable work is their bottleneck** — if they blame lead generation instead, the entire product is aimed at the wrong problem.
2. **They will pay $199–$499 for a system rather than software** — if they only buy subscriptions, the product must be repackaged.
3. **A system is perceived as meaningfully different from a prompt pack** — if not, AUREVIA is priced 10× above a category it cannot escape.
4. **Team adoption is achievable** — if only the owner ever uses it, the value proposition collapses and refunds follow.
5. **They will buy from an unknown brand with no case studies** — the hardest and most binary assumption.
6. **They are reachable organically via LinkedIn/YouTube** without paid spend.

Test methods and pass/fail thresholds are in `validation/validation-plan.md`.
