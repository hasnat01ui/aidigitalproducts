## The central gap

> Small agencies have **access** to AI and no **method** for it. The market sells them engines and it sells them consultants. Nobody sells them the operating manual at a price they will pay.

Everything below is a facet of that.

---

## Gap 1 — The $50-to-$5,000 void

**Observed:** the market splits into template products around $9–79 and done-for-you consultancies at $3,000–8,000/month with $3,000–10,000 setup fees. Between them, almost nothing.

**Why it exists:** templates are cheap to make and cheap to sell, so everyone makes them. Consultancies need high prices to cover human delivery. The middle requires the effort of a real system with none of the per-customer labour — which is hard, and therefore rare.

**AUREVIA's move:** occupy the middle deliberately at $199–$499. Priced like a product, built like a system, delivered with zero marginal labour.

**Risk:** the middle may be empty because buyers refuse to sit there — they may want cheap or want done-for-you. **This is validation assumption #2 and it is the one most likely to be wrong.**

---

## Gap 2 — Tools are sold; methods are not

**Observed:** every competitor sells capability. ChatGPT sells generation. Notion AI sells generation inside Notion. Templates sell fragments. None sells *the sequence a specific business follows to get a specific outcome repeatedly.*

**Why it exists:** methods require domain knowledge. A general tool company cannot afford to be agency-specific; a template seller does not have the depth.

**AUREVIA's move:** sell the method, and make the method the product. The unique mechanism is the workflow chain — input, prompt, review gate, output standard — not the prompt text.

**Why this is defensible:** a prompt is copyable in seconds. A validated end-to-end method with quality gates and adoption design is not, and it improves with every customer.

---

## Gap 3 — Nothing is built for team adoption

**Observed:** AI products are sold to individuals. Even team-priced tools are individually used. Every template product assumes one reader.

**The real buyer problem:** the agency owner does not need *themselves* to use AI better. They need their *team* to produce consistent output without the owner reviewing everything. That is the actual bottleneck named in the avatar.

**AUREVIA's move:** design for the team from the start — role-based workflows, a shared quality standard, review gates, onboarding for staff, and a way to tell whether it is being used.

**Why this matters commercially:** it justifies the price gap over a $49 template (which serves one person), and it directly answers the "will my team actually use it?" objection, which is the top adoption risk in the avatar.

---

## Gap 4 — Everything is model-locked or model-naive

**Observed:** products are either tied to one vendor (custom GPTs, Notion AI) or ignore the question entirely (prompt packs that assume GPT-4-era behaviour and quietly rot).

**Why it matters:** models change every few months. An agency that standardised on one vendor last year is already re-doing the work. A product that breaks on the next model release generates refunds.

**AUREVIA's move:** model-agnostic by design — the system works with whatever assistant the agency already pays for — plus an explicit maintenance commitment. "Updated as models change" is both a real differentiator and the natural justification for the Aurevia Pro recurring tier.

---

## Gap 5 — Non-billable work is nobody's product

**Observed:** the tooling market is aimed at *producing the deliverable* — writing, design, code. Proposals, scoping, onboarding, status reports, QA, and recaps are treated as admin overhead nobody has productised.

**Why it exists:** it is unglamorous. It does not demo well.

**Why it is the opportunity:** it is 25–40% of the week, it is entirely language work (maximum AI leverage), it is highly repeatable across clients (so a system generalises), and it is *low-risk to automate* — a bad first draft of an internal onboarding checklist costs nothing, whereas a bad client deliverable costs the account.

**AUREVIA's move:** make non-billable work the beachhead. It is the highest-leverage, lowest-risk, least-contested entry point, and it produces a measurable number (hours recovered) that makes the ROI argument without hype.

---

## Gap 6 — No trustworthy proof of outcome

**Observed:** the category is saturated with unverifiable claims — "10x your output", "save 40 hours a week". Buyers have been trained to discount all of it.

**AUREVIA's move:** measure and publish honestly. Give buyers a time-audit instrument as part of the free resource so they measure their own baseline, then measure again after. Real numbers from real customers, or no numbers.

**Constraint that makes this work:** the brief forbids fabricated testimonials, revenue, and scarcity. In a market this noisy, that restriction is an **advantage** — verifiable modesty is differentiating when everyone else is shouting.

---

## Gap 7 — The under-served buyer nobody markets to

**Observed:** AI tooling markets to enterprises (big budgets) and to individual creators (big volume). The 2–15 person agency is too small for enterprise sales motion and too serious for creator-tier products.

**AUREVIA's move:** speak to that buyer exclusively and in their own vocabulary — retainers, scope creep, utilisation, non-billables, account management. Specificity is the cheapest available differentiator, and it is free.

## The positioning these gaps produce

> **AUREVIA gives small agencies the operating method for AI — the workflows, standards, and review gates that turn tools they already pay for into consistent client work — without hiring, and without a consultancy retainer.**

## Gaps deliberately not pursued

- **Building an AI agency platform.** Requires funding and a long build; competes with well-capitalised incumbents.
- **A vertical AI SaaS.** Right destination (Aurevia OS), wrong starting point. Validate the method with digital products first, then encode it in software.
- **Serving enterprises.** Better budgets, but a sales motion a solo founder cannot run.
